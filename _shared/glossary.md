# Glossary — Austin TX Residential Real Estate

> Terms Diana's team uses daily. If a specialist encounters one of these
> in a handoff card, this is what it means. If you're adapting this system
> for another market, swap this glossary for your local terms.

---

## Texas-Specific Terms

**Option Period**
A negotiated window (typically 7-10 days) after contract execution where the buyer can terminate for ANY reason and lose only the option fee (usually $200-$500). This is Texas-specific — most states don't have it. It's the buyer's "try before you buy" period. Inspections happen here. This is the #1 deadline the Transaction Coordinator tracks.

**Option Fee**
A small fee ($200-$500) paid by the buyer directly to the seller at contract execution. It buys the option period. Non-refundable if the buyer terminates. Credited toward purchase price at closing if the deal goes through.

**Earnest Money**
A larger deposit ($3,000-$10,000+ depending on price) deposited with the title company within 1-3 business days of contract execution. Shows the buyer is serious. Refundable if the buyer terminates within allowed contingency periods. At risk if the buyer backs out without cause after contingencies expire.

**TREC (Texas Real Estate Commission)**
The state regulatory body. TREC forms are the standard contract forms used in most residential transactions. Diana's team uses TREC contracts.

**Seller's Disclosure**
A TREC-required document where the seller discloses known property conditions, defects, and history. Buyers review this early — it often surfaces issues that affect negotiation. Not a substitute for an inspection.

**MUD (Municipal Utility District)**
A special taxing district common in suburban Austin (78748, 78749, Round Rock). Adds $0.50-$1.50+ per $100 of assessed value to property taxes. Buyers are often surprised by this. Always flag MUD status in property research.

## Transaction Terms

**DOM (Days on Market)**
How long a property has been actively listed. Low DOM (under 7) = competitive, expect multiple offers. High DOM (over 45) = price may be negotiable. Average in Austin varies by zip code.

**CMA (Comparative Market Analysis)**
A report comparing a property to similar recently sold properties. Used to recommend list price (for sellers) or offer price (for buyers). Property Research produces these.

**Escalation Clause**
A clause in an offer that automatically increases the buyer's offer price up to a cap if competing offers exist. Common in Austin's competitive zip codes (78704, 78702). Example: "Offer $535K, escalate by $2K above highest competing offer up to $560K."

**Clear to Close**
The lender has completed underwriting and approved the loan. All conditions are met. This is the green light. The Transaction Coordinator celebrates internally when this arrives.

**VOE (Verification of Employment)**
Lender verifies the buyer is still employed 48-72 hours before closing. If the buyer lost their job or changed jobs, the deal can fall apart at the last minute. TC flags this as a final checkpoint.

## Austin Market Terms

**78704, 78702, 78701** — Central/South Austin. Walkable, trendy, expensive. Bidding wars common. Foundation issues in pre-1970 builds. Zilker, Barton Hills, Travis Heights, SoCo.

**78745, 78748, 78749** — South/Southwest Austin. Suburban, family-oriented. Better value per sqft. School quality varies by specific attendance zone. MUD districts common in 78748/78749.

**78731, 78757** — Northwest Austin. Established neighborhoods, good schools, access to Domain/Arboretum. Less volatile pricing.

**78721, 78722, 78702** — East Austin. Rapidly appreciating. Gentrification dynamics. Investment buyers active. Duplex/ADU opportunities. Cultural significance — handle conversations about neighborhood change with care.
